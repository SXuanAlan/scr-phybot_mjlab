Fig. X. 3D bodies of the equivalent pad limit surface under per-unit
moment billing (top row), each carrying the full rake-shear axis including
the against-rake (reverse) residual, with their f_t = 70 N cross-sections
below (bottom row; the dashed ring on each body marks the cut, 70 N being
the per-hand hang shear). Red dots are measured release wrenches from the
GPU-rollout validation (rays lying in each slice; cache of
rollout_pad_wrench_surface.py). All at the F0 = 400 N DR floor; every
extent scales linearly with F0 in [400, 650] N. (a, d) Force body at zero
moment in (f_t, f_l, f_n): the closed Wang-2017 surface at pad scale
(identical to the aggregate F0*R by the zero-moment collapse), colored by
tension. Capacity peaks along the rake (400 N), lateral holds 0.57 F0,
tension exists only alongside shear (dome height 48 N at ~15 deg
elevation), and the against-rake side collapses to the ~17 N side-contact
residual; the r_floor scrap cap (0.02 F0) closes the surface where the
deterministic model predicts zero. Only the tension half is drawn —
compression (f_n < 0) rides the anchor for free, so the (d) slice region
is open downward; the x marks the hang operating point (f_l = 0,
f_n = 14 N), a 3.3x capacity multiple inside the boundary. (b, e) Bending
body in (f_t, m_l, m_t): the bending-interaction diamonds lofted along
shear, pinching to the reverse capacity on the left and the pure-shear
capacity at 400 N on the right, widest near f_t = 0.43 F0 (1.18 /
1.28 N*m); the (e) slice is the near-linear diamond (corner units
accumulate both bending tensions). (c, f) Twist-pitch body in
(f_t, m_n, m_l): an order of magnitude wider in m_n (peak 8.6 N*m)
because twist loads the units in-plane against the strong shear
direction. Its (f) slice is a bowtie with flat plateau wings: near pure
twist, small pitch moments ride under other units' budgets at no cost
(occupancy flat to |m_l| ~ 0.2 N*m at m_n = 4 N*m) and then a different
unit's tension takes over in a cliff. Cross-sections and slice panels are
grid contours (the fixed-shear slices are affine, not radial, cuts);
bodies are lofted from per-shear contour rings sharing a seam. Slice
release points are measured with two-phase rays (shear preload held at
70 N, moments ramped), pinning the release into the slice — a
proportional ray drifting ~1 % near the plateau-cliff would land on the
6D surface but 10-25 % past the slice in f_t; the residual outward bias
of the (f) wing points is the 3-step release debounce riding the cliff.
